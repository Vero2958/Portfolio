Voxel-wise encoding model on NSD data



Project Description


This is a visual neuroscience project trying to answer a practical question: can we predict a new subject's brain responses to images accurately when we only have a small amount of that subject's fMRI data?

The project revolves around developing a data-efficient transfer-learning approach to predict voxel-wise activations of a novel subject from other subjects' pretrained full-data models and the target's intrinsic rest-derived connectivity.

After implementing Gu et al., 2022, I found out their approach didn't perform as well as the full-data baseline on a voxel-wise task.

I performed a systematic error analysis to figure out what the problem was. Looks like OLS regressions do not like multicollinear inputs, and our stacking is REALLY multicollinear, so I fixed the approach by adding L2 regularization.

A personalization limitation noticed by the authors of the original paper gave me the idea of trying to personalize the approach by using intrinsic functional connectivity. Why? Well, because it is subject-specific and shaped by the activity through plasticity. Most importantly, it is available in the NSD :)

Soooo, I derived connectivity fingerprints from resting-state recordings from each subject and analyzed them to ensure that they contain information specific to the subject and correlated in some way with task activity, as the literature on the topic suggests they do.

Turns out rest-derived and task-derived connectivity seem to be highly correlated, so the first can potentially be used to predict the second. Importantly, there's no correlation between rest-derived connectivity fingerprints of different subjects.

This portion of the project is still ongoing, but I got some very interesting neuroscientific insights from some pieces of analysis I performed out of curiosity. For instance, I've found out that voxels have almost no functional correspondence between subjects. Between two subjects' V1v areas, only up to approximately 25% of voxels had a functionally similar twin in the other subject's region, and for later visual areas it was much less as well (less than 10%).

I also found out that, while the specific activation patterns during resting state were completely uncorrelated between sessions, the connectivity itself was well preserved over time, especially in early visual areas such as V1v. This means that really different patterns of activity can emerge from the same underlying functional connectivity.

Currently, I'm focusing on trying to express some of the information represented in the fingerprints in a meaningful way for the model. Preliminary results suggest that there's quite some margin of improvement for the connectivity to contribute. I'm also in the process of drafting a pre-print about the regularization fix (please send coffee). The rest will go in my bachelor's thesis.
